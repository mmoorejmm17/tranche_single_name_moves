- Debt (9/30/26): short-term $157M + long-term $4,063M = **$4,220M**. Tariff monetization financing liability: $81M (separate line).
- 9M FCF / total debt: (147) / 4,220 = **(3.5)%**.

- Guidance (Q3 release): FCF $50-100M, **including the $80M tariff receivable sale**; adjusted operating income $450-475M, including a $105M tariff benefit. Prior guidance (Q2): FCF ~$250M, adjusted operating income $555-605M.
- Implied Q4 FCF: if the company counts the $80M tariff cash, 9M FCF on that basis is about $(67)M, so guidance of $50-100M implies roughly **$117-167M in Q4**, versus $11M in Q2 and $7M in Q3. Estimate only; the company's own YTD FCF basis was not verified.
- Dividends on common and preferred suspended (Q3 release).

## 5. Flags

- Q3 figures are from the earnings release, not a 10-Q. Re-check when the 10-Q is filed (expected early November).
- GAAP puts the $80M tariff receivable sale in financing. Management's CFO and FCF guidance includes it. Always show both.
- Q2 EBITDA includes a $105M non-cash tariff cost recovery (reduction in cost of sales). Excluding it, Q2 EBITDA is about $155M.
- Lease book (finance receivables) released $152M of cash over 9M. That flatters CFO and cannot continue if the book keeps shrinking.
- Q3 working capital was roughly neutral (+3), so the gap between profit and cash in Q3 comes from interest ($93M), restructuring cash ($20M) and pension contributions ($46M).
- Adjusted operating income ($124M in Q3) excludes interest, restructuring and pension. Adjusted operating income less interest = about $31M, which is close to Q3 CFO of $30M.
- Stock comp is small (9M: $29M), so FCF after SBC is not materially different.
- Reclassification: non-financing interest expense was previously reported in "Other expenses (income), net" (noted in the Q2 10-Q). Prior-year comparisons in older filings may differ.
